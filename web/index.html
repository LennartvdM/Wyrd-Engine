Wyrd Engine

Synthetic people on a schedule, for testing agents against. Every version stays up and runnable, oldest first, so the reasoning is visible rather than polished away.

Each strip below is the same Tuesday — 200 people, seed 1, ten-minute bins — drawn from that version's own code. The differences between them are the model, not the drawing.

Workbench prototype

Where this started. One week of schedules per person from an explicit configuration, with a batch runner, a replay wheel and a Python console.

Durations were inflated by a multiplier applied to every activity including sleep, activities were placed end to end, days were boxed at midnight, and people were simulated independently of each other.

flock mk1 frozen

A population that keeps its own commitments. Households eat together, workplaces share a start time, and the commute waves come out of that rather than being written down.

Everyone was a metronome: a person arrived at work within 1.9 minutes of the same clock time every day of the year, and the page counted the population by the hour, which cannot show the shape inside an hour at all.

flock mk2 frozen

People stop keeping the same minutes. Each morning they draw the margin they leave themselves, and a journey can run long, so nobody's arrival is predictable to the minute.

Arrival spread went from 1.9 to 8.9 minutes and the morning ramp lost its spikes. But the schedule underneath was still fixed: the same start and the same length every weekday, so nobody ever stayed late or got away early.

flock mk3 current

The working day itself moves. Start and length are drawn per day, so the skeleton of a person's week stops repeating and the population's day blurs at every edge.

Work start now varies by about 10 minutes and length by about an hour within the same person. The population view counts a named day in five-minute bins, so what is being pooled is stated rather than left to the reader.
